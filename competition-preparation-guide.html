Preparation

How to Prepare for a Student Competition

Great results rarely come from talent alone — they come from smart, steady preparation. This practical guide walks students, parents, and teachers through a simple, proven approach to getting competition-ready with confidence.

A Simple Roadmap

Five Steps to Competition-Ready

Preparation is far less daunting when you break it into clear stages. Follow these five and you will walk in feeling ready.

1. Understand the Brief

Read the competition page and its published criteria carefully. Know exactly what is being asked, how it is judged, and what the deadlines are before you write a single word.

2. Plan Your Time

Work backwards from the deadline and set small milestones. A simple schedule turns a big challenge into a series of manageable steps.

3. Practise Deliberately

Practise the actual skill the competition tests, and do it against the published criteria so every attempt moves you closer to your best work.

4. Review & Refine

Step back, re-read the criteria, and improve the weakest parts. A second and third pass is where good entries become great ones.

5. Submit With Confidence

Check the format and requirements, submit before the deadline, and take pride in what you have created. You have already grown by taking part.

What Works

Habits of Successful Participants

Across categories and age groups, the students who do best tend to share the same simple habits.

They Read the Criteria First

Before creating anything, they know precisely how their work will be judged — and they keep the criteria beside them throughout.

They Practise Consistently

Short, regular sessions beat occasional marathons. Consistency builds skill and keeps stress low.

They Seek Feedback

They share drafts with a teacher, parent, or peer and act on the advice they receive.

They Focus on Quality

They would rather do one thing exceptionally well than many things adequately.

They Manage Deadlines

They submit early, leaving room to double-check the format and avoid last-minute problems.

They Learn From Every Attempt

Win or not, they use the performance summary and their own reflection to come back stronger.

Stay Balanced

Wellbeing Comes First

Preparation should build a student up, never wear them down. Keep the experience healthy and enjoyable by remembering to:

  • Set a realistic schedule with regular breaks
  • Prioritise sleep, meals, and time away from screens
  • Treat participation itself as the win
  • Ask for help early when something feels difficult
  • Celebrate progress, not just the final result

Parents and schools can find more guidance on the Parent Guide and Safety pages.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

How far in advance should I start preparing?

Give yourself as much runway as the format allows — even a week of steady, focused practice beats a last-minute rush. Start by reading the competition brief and its published criteria, explained on our judging process page.

What is the single most useful preparation habit?

Practising against the actual criteria. When you know exactly how your work will be assessed, every hour of practice is aimed in the right direction. Our free resources can help you plan.

How do I stay calm and balanced while preparing?

Keep a realistic schedule, take regular breaks, and remember that taking part is already an achievement. Our safety guidance and Parent Guide cover healthy, balanced participation.

Put Your Preparation to Work

Choose your next challenge and apply what you have learned. Every competition is a chance to grow.
