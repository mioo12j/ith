Every student deserves a safe, respectful place to compete. Here is how we protect participants and what we ask of families and schools.

Safer by Design

What “Online-First” Means for Safety

The way our competitions run removes many of the risks that in-person events carry.

1

Because Inspire Talent Hub competitions are online-first, students take part from the safety of their own home or their own school, under the supervision of a parent or teacher. There is no travel to unfamiliar venues, no unsupervised gatherings, and no physical contact with strangers. For a parent, this is one of the quiet advantages of the format: your child can compete on a national stage without ever leaving a place you trust.

2

The platform is also built so that taking part never requires a student to expose themselves publicly. A student does not need a public profile, does not broadcast their work to strangers, and is not placed into open chat with unknown adults or other children. Competing is a private matter between the student and the evaluation process — not a social network, and not a stage for unwanted attention.

3

This design is deliberate. Any feature that could create a safety risk — open messaging, public leaderboards tied to a child’s identity, unmoderated interaction — is either avoided or carefully controlled. We would rather build fewer features and keep every young participant safe than add engagement at the cost of a child’s wellbeing.

Safeguarding

Protecting Younger Participants

Students are children first and competitors second, and we treat them that way.

1

We ask that younger students take part with the involvement of a parent, guardian, or teacher — someone who helps them register, understands what they are entering, and is there if a question or concern arises. This is not a barrier to participation; it is a safeguard, and it is also the arrangement in which a young student competes with the most confidence. A supported child is a safer and a happier competitor.

2

Competition briefs, prompts, and materials are chosen to be age-appropriate for school students, and reviewers are expected to handle every entry with the care due to a young person’s work. A student’s submission is treated as private and is never displayed publicly, mocked, or shared without consent. The dignity of the child who created the work is part of what safety means to us — not only protection from harm, but respect for the person.

Data & Privacy

Careful With Your Information

We collect as little as possible, use it only for what you expect, and protect it.

1

Our approach to data is minimalist by principle: we ask only for the information genuinely needed to run a competition and issue a verifiable certificate, and we use it only for those purposes. We do not treat a child’s data as a product to be traded. Exactly what we collect, why, and how it is handled is set out plainly in our Privacy Policy and Cookie Policy, written to be read and understood rather than to hide behind legal language.

2

Parts of the platform are designed so that no personal data needs to leave your device at all. The Study Hub, for example, runs entirely in your browser: your practice history, bookmarks, and progress are stored locally on your own device, not on our servers. It is a small illustration of a larger philosophy — keep data close to the user, collect only what is necessary, and never ask for more than the task requires.

Stay Alert

Accounts, Credentials & Avoiding Scams

A few simple habits keep students safe from impersonation and fraud.

We Never Ask for Passwords

Inspire Talent Hub will never ask for your password or a one-time code by email, phone, or message. Anyone who does is not us — do not share it.

Use Official Channels Only

Register, log in, and contact us only through our official website. Be cautious of look-alike pages, forwarded links, or messages promising guaranteed prizes.

Verify Certificates Properly

Confirm any certificate only through our official verification page, using its unique ID or QR code. A genuine certificate can always be checked there.

Keep Details Private

Keep registration and login details to yourself, and let a parent or teacher help younger students manage them. Report anything that looks like impersonation.

Respect

A Fair, Respectful Community

Safety is not only about data and scams — it is also about how people treat each other.

1

Every participant is entitled to compete free from harassment, intimidation, or unfair play. We expect courtesy and honesty from everyone who takes part, and our Code of Conduct sets out clearly what is expected and what is not tolerated. Behaviour that would make a competition unsafe or unwelcoming for a young person has no place here.

2

Fair play is part of safety too. The same integrity safeguards that make our judging process fair — published criteria, independent review, and firm action against plagiarism — also protect honest students from being cheated of the recognition they earned. A safe competition is one where doing the right thing is also the winning thing.

If Something Is Wrong

How to Report a Concern

If anything ever feels unsafe or unfair, tell us — we would always rather hear from you.

1

If you encounter anything that worries you — a suspicious message, a possible impersonation, content that seems inappropriate, or a concern about fairness or safety — contact us promptly through our Contact page with as much detail as you can give. Parents, teachers, and students are all equally welcome to raise a concern, and no concern is too small to report.

2

We take reports seriously and respond as quickly as we reasonably can. We will look into what you have told us honestly, take appropriate action, and, where it is appropriate to do so, let you know the outcome. Raising a genuine concern will never count against a participant — it is exactly the right thing to do, and it helps us keep the platform safe for everyone.

In an emergency, or if a child is in immediate danger, always contact your local emergency services or child-protection helpline first. Then let us know, so we can help in any way we can.

Common Questions

Safety, Answered

The questions parents and schools most often ask about safe participation.

Is it safe for my child to take part?

Yes. Competitions are online-first, so your child takes part from home or school under your or a teacher’s supervision, with no travel and no unsupervised contact with strangers. There is no public profile and no open chat with unknown people — participation is private between the student and the evaluation process.

What information do you collect about my child?

Only what is genuinely needed to run a competition and issue a verifiable certificate. We do not sell or trade personal data. The full detail is in our Privacy Policy, and parts of the site, such as the Study Hub, keep progress on your own device rather than on a server.

Will my child interact with strangers online?

No. The platform does not place students into open messaging or public interaction with unknown adults or other children. Taking part does not require broadcasting work to strangers or maintaining a public profile.

How do I know a certificate or message is genuinely from you?

Verify any certificate only through our official verification page using its unique ID or QR code. We will never ask for your password or a one-time code, and you should only ever register or log in through our official website.

How do I report a safety concern?

Contact us through our Contact page with as much detail as possible. Anyone — student, parent, or teacher — can raise a concern, we take every report seriously, and reporting will never count against a participant. In an emergency, contact local emergency services first.
